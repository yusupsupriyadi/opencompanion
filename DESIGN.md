# DESIGN.md: OpenCompanion

This design direction is transcribed from the reference image the owner provided (a late-afternoon meadow painting, a butter-cream sky, a hiker in a blue jacket sitting on a log, a pixel ground line across the middle). This document is the design data for building screens in Pencil and later in Svelte. Feature requirements are in `docs/PRD.md`.

## 1. Identity

- **Product**: OpenCompanion, a local desktop app for running AI coding CLIs, giving them tasks, and monitoring them.
- **Personality**: calm, warm, dependable. Like sitting on a hill watching over a field: you do not need to stare at the terminal all the time, the app tells you when something needs you.
- **Design Read**: a local control room for developers who run several AI CLIs at once, with a pastoral painterly visual language plus pixel line accents.
- **Dial**: ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1.
  - ENERGY 2: illustration and warm colors give character, daily work screens stay calm.
  - RHYTHM 2: a consistent app structure (sidebar + work area), broken up by illustrated moments (onboarding, empty state, mobile offline).
  - MOTION 1: only hover, focus, and transitions when a status changes. No looping animation. Exception at the owner's request (2026-09-25): the title of a Running session in the sidebar has a `--rainbow` gradient that shifts slowly (4 seconds per cycle) and stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Second exception at the owner's request (2026-09-27), phone only: the dot in the status chip breathes while the CLI works and rings while it waits for you, the "to latest output" button and newly shown panels slide in, buttons shrink slightly when pressed, sheets rise from the bottom; all of it is off under `prefers-reduced-motion` (section 11).
- **One focus per screen**: every screen has one main element (see sections 9 and 10). On the Overview that is the "Needs you" panel.

- Without haze, `ink` text over the trees, the log, and the dark grass drops to about 1:1. So every block of text over the painting sits on a glass **plate** with radius 10: the page header, the Skills toolbar, Planner replies, the session side panel, and "Session not found" with padding 12/16; section headers, the Activity row, helper text, and notes with padding 8/12. The page header, the Activity row, and panels span their row; section headers, helper text, notes, and Planner replies hug their text.
- Text on a plate uses `ink` for secondary text too, so the hierarchy is carried by size and weight.
- **Liquid glass** (the owner's decision, 26 September 2026, replacing the rule of glass only in the sidebar and the antislop R-10 limit of one or two elements): the sidebar, the window buttons, all text plates, cards, and panels (session rows, Needs you, state box, chat history, composer, live rail, message bubbles, Settings cards, tables, the Onboarding panel, dialogs, toasts) use `glass-blur` and `glass-rim`. Inside them `surface`, `surface-2`, and `bg` are replaced by `glass`, `glass-2`, and `glass-bg`, so secondary buttons, inputs, selects, segmented controls, options, and rows turn transparent too; inputs and secondary buttons also get `glass-rim`. Primary buttons, status chips, and the terminal stay solid.
- Each tint is the thinnest one that still keeps `ink-2` at 4.5:1 over the blurred painting. Pop-ups (dialogs, the pop-up version of the live rail, the @ folder list) float outside their parent glass, so nothing is blurred behind them, and they use `glass-pop`, which passes without blur. The `st-err` red needs 89% behind it, so error text (`.err-text`, field messages) uses a small `solid` plate.
- The chat history and the live rail docked in Chat are as tall as the sidebar, so both use `glass-thin` so they do not read as a second sidebar (the owner's request, 26 September 2026). A tint this thin passes only with `ink`, so secondary text inside them is `ink` too and the hierarchy is carried by size and weight.
- Dusk: brightness .58 with 38% haze keeps `ink` at 5.07:1 even directly over the painting, so the painting's colors show without giving up that contrast margin.
- Cards, chips, the terminal, and tables stay solid.

## 4. Spacing, grid, shape

- Spacing scale: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 22, 24, 28, 32, 40.
- Reference desktop window 1440 × 900. The minimum that must stay tidy: 1100 × 700 (the Live rail in Chat and the right column of sessions opened outside are hidden below 1280 and can be opened with a button; the right panel of Session detail stays on the right at every width).
- Frame: a window without a native frame; the top row, 36 tall, is the Title bar (section 8), then padding 12 on the left, right, and bottom and a gap of 12. The sidebar is a glass panel with radius 14; the work area has no panel, so the painting shows, and its contents are glass cards or text on glass plates (section 2). The Live rail in Chat is a glass panel of its own.
- Sidebar 240 wide, padding 22/16, gap between blocks 22.
- Main area: padding 28 top and bottom, 40 left and right, gap between blocks 24 to 28.
- Reference mobile 390 × 844. Side padding 16, gap 12 to 16, touch targets at least 44 × 44, main action button 48 tall.
- Radius: `r-sm` 6 (chips, inputs, code boxes), `r-btn` 8 (buttons, nav items, option cards), `r-md` 10 (cards, tables, terminal), `r-lg` 14 (Needs you panel, modals, onboarding panel). Only the switch is pill shaped.
- Border: 1px `line` for panels; 1px `line-strong` for inputs and secondary buttons; 1.5px `accent` only for the Needs you panel; 2px `forest` for a selected option card.
- Shadow only on modals: offset 0/12, blur 40, `#33361F40`. Every other element is flat.
- Glass means liquid glass on every card, panel, dialog, and toast (section 2). Chips and the terminal do not use it. No glow, decorative gradients, or background grids.

## 6. Identity motif: the horizon line

The session activity track, taken from the pixel ground line in the reference.

- Size 220 × 16 (a free-position frame).
- Ground: a 1px `line-strong` line at y 11 across the full width.
- Specks: 12 boxes of 2 × 1 `line-strong` under the line (alternating y 14 or 15), unevenly spaced like grains of soil.
- Events: 4 × 4 boxes above the line (y 7). Color `ink-2` for commands and file edits, `st-wait` for permission prompts, `st-err` for errors. The x position follows the event's time within the visible range.
- Now: a 3 × 8 `forest` box at the right end, shown only while the session is Running. It does not blink; it shifts when a new event arrives.
- Used in: session rows (Overview), the Live rail in Chat, mobile session cards. The desktop Session detail no longer uses it (the Activity row was removed, 2026-09-28).

## 11. Motion

- Hover and focus: 120 ms ease-out color transition.
- Status chip changes: 200 ms crossfade. A row moving into the Needs you panel: 200 ms slide, no bounce.
- Modal: 150 ms scrim fade + the modal rises 8px in 180 ms.

- Phone (the owner's exception, 2026-09-27): the chip dot breathes (opacity 1 to 0.3, 1.6 seconds) during Starting or Running and rings 4px (1.4 seconds) during Waiting for you, and stays still in other statuses; buttons and key caps shrink to 92% when pressed (100 ms), cards to 98.5%; a newly shown panel rises 4px while fading in (180 ms); sheets rise 24px (220 ms); the active tab line widens in 200 ms; jumping to the latest output scrolls smoothly.

## 12. Accessibility

- Contrast follows the table in section 2. New pairs are checked first.
- Status is never color alone: chips are always labelled. Session items in the sidebar show only the CLI logo, the title and the count of running CLIs; the CLI, the status and the names of running CLIs are still read by screen readers and are in the tooltip.
- Every control can be reached with Tab in visual order; Enter/Space activates; Esc closes modals and sheets.
- Focus ring 2px `forest` + 2px offset in both themes.
- An action that removes its own button (Approve, Run, Discard, a dialog whose opener is gone) moves focus to the first control that replaces it, or to the page's main area; focus never falls to the start of the document. The pairing code countdown is not read out every second; its expiry is announced once.
- Terminal: xterm with screen reader mode on; a summary of the session status is also announced through a live region ("Codex CLI in ai-remote is waiting for you"). While the CLI runs, Tab belongs to the CLI, so Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab move focus out of the terminal (mentioned in the terminal's footer note); a closed terminal does not capture Tab at all. In the session terminal, Shift+Enter adds a line (sent as Alt+Enter, which AI CLIs read as a new line) and, on Windows, Ctrl+V pastes as in Windows Terminal: text as it is, an image as a temporary file path that the CLI attaches as an image. That hint is not shown under the session terminal (the owner's decision, 2026-09-30), but stays for screen readers: "Type straight into the terminal. Shift+Enter adds a line. Ctrl+V pastes text or an image. Ctrl+C interrupts the CLI. When {cli} exits, type {command} to start it again, or exit to close the terminal. Ctrl+Tab leaves the terminal." Shells in tabs still show their own note.
- Mobile: text can be enlarged to 200% without being cut off; a focused input is not covered by the keyboard.
- Desktop: Settings › Text size (90, 100, 110, 125, 150%) uses webview zoom, so the scale in section 3 and the spacing in section 4 grow together. In a narrow window the layout drops to the existing 1100 and 720 breakpoints.

## 15. Decisions (one line per decision)

- Cream + olive: taken straight from the reference's sky and text, giving a warmth that sets it apart from the usual dark developer tools.
- Forest green as primary: the darkest, steadiest color in the painting, with enough contrast for buttons.
- Jacket blue as the only accent: in the painting the hiker is the focal point; in the app the focal moment is a session waiting for you.
- Light as default + Dusk: the reference is daytime; Dusk exists because developers often work at night, and the toggle must work in both themes.
- The terminal is always dark: CLI output and ANSI colors need a dark background to be readable.
- Nunito: its rounded letter terminals match the reference title, and it stays clear at size 13.
- Pixelify Sans only for small labels: one pixel echo of the reference's ground line, kept to one place so it does not become a gimmick.
- Sidebar + work area: the app is used for hours with five main destinations, and the sidebar keeps each one a click away.
- Session rows, not grid cards: sessions are compared with each other (status, duration, activity), and rows with aligned columns are easier to scan.
- Brand logos in CliMark: the owner asked for them, and a logo makes the CLI recognizable at once in the session list. The monogram is only a fallback while there is no logo.
- Illustrations only in moments without data: there is nothing to read there, so the painting carries the identity without getting in the way of work.
- Skills as a folder × skill matrix, not a list per CLI: the screen's goal is comparison, and aligned columns make gaps visible at once. The columns are folders because which CLI reads which folder depends on the CLI version.
- Skills only gives copy commands and never copies by itself: like the CLI install rule, OpenCompanion does not write to CLI config folders.
- Pixel hill logo (chosen by the owner on 2026-09-27 from three concepts: Hiker, Horizon line, Pixel hill): the smooth hill from the painting plus a pixel hiker from the reference's ground line, still readable at 16 px. Jacket blue may appear in the logo because the hiker is the source of the accent color; on work screens the accent stays reserved for needs-you moments. The `OC` monogram is not used because it is already OpenCode's fallback logo in CliMark.
- A tabbed session panel (Files, Changes, Branch, Details) in the right column, not a new screen: the owner wants to see the folder, diff and branch while watching the terminal, as in Orca. Everything is read-only except Switch branch (the owner's choice), and desktop only so file contents never travel over LAN HTTP to the phone.
- The viewer in a dark tab above the terminal: code and diffs need the same dark background as the terminal, and the shell tab strip is already the visual language for "a dark panel with a lid".
- Syntax colors from the Dusk `rainbow` list and the terminal colors, not a popular editor theme: the colors already come from the painting and already pass contrast on a dark background, so the viewer still feels part of the app. Diffs are colored per side (unchanged and added lines as the new file, removed lines as the old file) so a comment or string left open on one side does not color the other. The app ships only about 40 language grammars common in projects, not all of Shiki's, because all of them add about 9 MB.
- Viewer tab icons colored by file type (the owner's decision, 2026-09-30), an exception to the rule that color is only for status and code: the colors come from the `rainbow` list that already passes contrast, and each type uses the color people already know (PDF red, JavaScript yellow, TypeScript blue), so there is no new color apart from the Day dark gold `ft-yellow`.
- PDFs drawn by pdf.js, not the webview's built-in PDF viewer: WebView2 and WKWebView have their own viewer but WebKitGTK on Linux does not, and pdf.js makes PDFs look the same on all three systems, in the same dark panel as code. Missing because of that: text search in PDFs, links inside PDFs, and CMaps for CJK PDFs whose fonts are not embedded.
- Plain shells as tabs in the session, not a Terminal screen of their own: the owner asked for the two to be merged (2026-09-28), like the terminal tabs per worktree in Orca. Shells always start in the session's folder, so the New terminal dialog no longer asks for a folder.
- Free, nested terminal splits: the owner first picked one direction per tab (2026-09-30), then, while trying the drag, asked for a layout that is "fleksibel dan bebas" (flexible and free). Split uses the New terminal dialog so choosing a shell stays one step, and the layout is only remembered while the window is open because its shells also stop when OpenCompanion closes.
- Terminals are positioned by the layout, not by nested containers: moving a terminal to another split does not move its element to another container, so xterm is never restarted and the Tab order stays the reading order.
- Terminal drag with our own pointer events, not HTML drag and drop: on Windows, Tauri holds the webview's drag and drop for dropped files, and pointer events give full control over the drop marks. Menus give a way without dragging: swap, tab edge, new tab, and split with another tab.
- Sessions without a header, and the session terminal sitting on a shell: the owner wants to exit and run the CLI again as in a plain terminal, without Resume, Stop and Mark done, and a more compact view because the sidebar already shows the session's name and status (2026-09-28).
- A session counts as running only while an AI CLI runs in its terminals, its shell tabs included (the owner's decision, 2026-09-30): the process list is read every 2 seconds, the same as the "×2" count in the sidebar. A terminal left at its shell prompt has the status "No CLI running" (idle chip, no gradient, not counted under Running on the Overview and the phone, sorted with finished sessions) and may be deleted; its shell and tabs close with it. While a CLI runs only in a shell tab, the session stays Running, but Chat cards are not typed into the session terminal, because there the shell would run the text as a command.
- Mobile with a bottom tab bar (Sessions, Chat): the owner asked for Chat and New session on the phone (2026-09-25), so the main destinations must be one tap away; the Board tab went away when Board was removed (2026-09-28); detail screens hide it because they use the bottom edge for the action bar.
- Automations are scheduled in the backend, not in the OS (the owner's request, 2026-09-30): one thread checks every 30 seconds while the app runs, including in the tray; a missed schedule runs once when the app is active again, marked Late. Every permission mode is allowed, Bypass too, with the same warning as New session.
- Chat creates automations through a card, not directly (the owner's request, 2026-09-30): the planner puts a recurring request in `automations` with a local cron, gets the list of existing automations and the current time, and nothing is scheduled before Create is pressed.
- The landing page uses jan.ai's structure (the owner's choice, 2026-09-30, replacing the scroll-driven one-day story; since 2026-09-30 it lives in the separate repo `opencompanion-landing` and is published by Vercel at opencompanion.vercel.app, no longer in `landing/` and GitHub Pages): a hero "Meet OpenCompanion" with a transparent nav inside the panel that becomes a solid bar on scroll, a Download button that recognizes Windows, Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel when the browser tells) or Linux and downloads the installer from the latest release right away, with an arrow button next to it that opens every installer per system (phones and unknown systems open that list directly), then Read the source, an app demo you can try that reaches deep into the hero's bottom edge (the owner's request, 2026-09-30: the sidebar switches to Chat, Automations, Settings and the session screen; Approve/Deny, Create automation, switches, the composer and the session panel tabs work on sample data, and every element that looks like a button does work; below 1024 the sidebar is replaced by a tab row), numbered feature rows whose titles stick while their right side scrolls, and a closing panel of the same meadow. The painting fills the panel (fill: edges may be cropped, never stretched) under a sky-blue veil `rgb(33 89 170 / .86)` that holds cream text at least 4.77:1 over the whitest cloud, then fades out over the meadow. Every section uses one `.wrap` container at most 1600px wide with the same gutter, so edges and text line up. Day theme only, with no toggle, and a white page background (`bg`/`surface` `#FFFFFF`, `surface-2` `#F2F4EC`, `line` `#E2E5D9`) instead of cream (the owner's decision); the app mockup keeps the app's Day cream. Buttons, cards and numbers have a 2px `ink` edge with a hard shadow, an exception to the modal-only shadow rule that applies only on the landing page. MOTION stays 1 (hover, focus, buttons pressing down when pressed).
